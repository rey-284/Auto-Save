Three coverages to know

What each one generally helps with. Exact coverage depends on the policy.

01 · OFTEN REQUIRED

Liability

Other people and their property. Can help pay when you’re legally responsible for an injury or damage. It generally won’t fix your own car.

02 · CRASH DAMAGE

Collision

Your car after a crash. Can help repair your car after hitting another car or an object. Your deductible usually comes first.

03 · NON-CRASH

Comprehensive

Some unexpected losses. Can help with covered theft, hail, or a fallen branch. Check exclusions and your deductible.

POCKET GLOSSARY

Terms, decoded.

The basics you’ll see on almost every quote.

Premium
What you pay to keep the policy active.
Deductible
Your share of a covered repair before insurance pays its share.
Limit
The most a coverage can pay for a covered loss.
Claim
A request for help with a loss under your policy.
Policy period
The dates your coverage is active.
THE QUOTE CHECKLIST

A low monthly price only helps if the policy fits. Use this list when you talk to an insurer or compare quotes.

QUICK ANSWERS

Student driver FAQ

What does a deductible mean?

It’s the amount you pay toward a covered claim before your insurer pays its share. Check which coverages have a deductible and choose an amount you could afford.

Can I drive my family’s car at school?

Ask the owner and insurer how the policy applies, especially if you drive regularly or keep the car at school. Rules depend on the policy, household, and state.

Do good grades lower my rate?

Some insurers offer a good-student discount, but eligibility and savings vary. Ask what proof is needed and compare the final quote.

What should I compare on two quotes?

Match the drivers, vehicle, liability limits, deductibles, and optional coverage first. Then compare the total premium and payment schedule.

What should I do after a crash?

Put safety first and contact emergency services if needed. When safe, exchange details, document what happened, and contact your insurer. Follow local requirements.
